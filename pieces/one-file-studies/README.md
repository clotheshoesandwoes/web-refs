## What makes them work

1. **One subject, one object, drawn in full.** The croissant, the turntable, the watch, the sardine tin. The object takes a third of the screen or more and nothing else competes with it.
2. **The palette comes from the subject.** Tin red and cream for the cannery, blueprint blue for the bicycle maker, newsprint for the paper. No page uses a generic dark theme with one accent.
3. **Type is chosen for the period and trade.** Blackletter masthead on the 1926 newspaper, wide grotesk on the concrete zine, a didone italic on the perfume. Headlines are large, often over 100 px.
4. **Controls are part of the object.** The tone arm starts the record, the elevator dial picks the floor, the lamp is the light switch. There are almost no free-standing buttons.
5. **Specific invented facts.** "Hand-packed in Porto Salgado since 1923", "Twenty-two frames a year", "Fermented 90 days". Numbers, places and dates make a page read as a real business. For a client these must be their real ones.
6. **One page, one screen idea.** Most say everything in the first screen and use the rest for detail.

## Picks for client pages

Fictional businesses, built like a real brand page. Closest to what a shop owner would buy.

## Don't steal

- The small spaced-out capital labels above headings. Nearly every page has them, and they are on the banned list.
- Invented history. A client page uses the owner's real dates, prices and words.
- Whole-page simulations (pond, aurora, attractors). Good toys, but a shop page needs hours, address and a phone number on the first screen.
- Drawing everything in CSS. Where a real photo of the shop exists, the photo wins.
